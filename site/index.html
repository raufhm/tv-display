How it works

A real extra screen, not a mirror.

macOS thinks you plugged in a monitor. It shows up in System Settings → Displays, and only what you put on it goes to the TV.

1

Your Mac grows a new screen

A 1280×720 display appears, ready for windows.

CGVirtualDisplay
2

Only that screen is filmed

Your main screen stays private.

ScreenCaptureKit
3

Squished into video

The Mac's video chip shrinks each frame in a blink.

H.264 · VideoToolbox
4

Zips over your Wi-Fi

If the TV falls behind, the Mac waits, so lag never piles up.

TCP on your LAN
5

The TV unsquishes it

Its own video chip draws each frame the moment it's ready.

Android MediaCodec

Install

Two apps, about five minutes.

first

On the TV

Android TV / Google TV 9 or later

  1. Install Downloader (by AFTVnews) from the Play Store on your TV.
  2. Open it and type this address: 
    this-site/tv
    When asked, let Downloader install unknown apps, then choose Install.
  3. Open TV Display once. After that it waits in the background and switches on by itself when your Mac connects. After a full TV restart it comes back by itself; on some TVs that takes a few minutes, or just open it once.
Prefer adb?

With developer options and network debugging on:

adb connect 192.168.1.20:5555
adb install TV-Display.apk

Download TV-Display.apk (97 KB)

then

On the Mac

macOS 14 Sonoma or later

  1. Download the .dmg and drag TV Display into Applications.
  2. Open it. macOS says it can't check the app: click Done, then go to System Settings → Privacy & Security and click Open Anyway. Or, in Terminal: 
    xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine "/Applications/TV Display.app"
  3. It finds your TV by itself. Allow it to find devices on your local network and to use Screen Recording when macOS asks.
  4. The TV icon fills in when it's streaming. Drag a window onto the new screen. Boing. Text too small from the sofa? Menu bar → Text Size → Larger.
